Notes on Starting ML Research

April 2026

Transitioning from competitive programming and Kaggle into actual ML research was a bigger jump than I expected. In competitions, the objective is clear — optimize a metric. In research, the hardest part is figuring out what question is worth asking in the first place.

The first thing that surprised me was how much reading is involved. Before writing a single line of code, I spent weeks going through related work. At first it felt inefficient, but I quickly realized that without a solid grasp of the landscape, you end up reinventing things that already exist.

My research at MANU focused on statistical methods applied to ML pipelines. The IEEE publication process was its own learning curve — peer review cycles, rebuttals, formatting requirements. But the feedback I received made the final paper substantially better than the initial submission.

For anyone starting ML research: find an advisor who gives you direction without micromanaging, read papers actively (take notes, reproduce key results), and don't be afraid to work on problems that seem small. Small problems done rigorously build the foundation for bigger contributions.
