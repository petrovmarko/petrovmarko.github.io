Insights from Competitive Programming

June 2026

Competitive programming has shaped how I think about problems more than any other activity. When I first started, I was overwhelmed by the sheer number of topics — graph theory, dynamic programming, number theory, combinatorics — and the speed at which top competitors seemed to solve them.

Over time, I realized that the key isn't knowing every algorithm. It's about pattern recognition: identifying the structure of a problem and mapping it to a technique you already know. The IOI taught me this lesson the hard way — HM was a proud result, but the real value was in the hundreds of hours of practice that made it possible.

The most underrated skill is debugging under time pressure. When you have 20 minutes left and your solution fails on the third test case, staying calm and methodical matters more than raw knowledge. I've lost count of how many times a simple off-by-one error cost me a full score.

My advice to anyone starting: pick a platform (Codeforces is my go-to), solve problems slightly above your current level, and review editorials even when you solve a problem. There's often a cleaner approach you missed.
